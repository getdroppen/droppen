## Droppen App for macOS

`Droppen-0.2.142.dmg` — the desktop app (Apple silicon and Intel), signed with Developer ID and notarised by Apple. It needs macOS 13 or later. Open the DMG, drag Droppen to Applications, open it.

Droppen 0.2.129 to 0.2.141 update themselves to this release: they download it in the background and install it the next time you quit, or at once with *Restart to update*. 0.2.133 and 0.2.134 freeze after an update check that finds nothing newer, but not after one that finds this release, so they update the same way; if yours is frozen now, force-quit it (Option-Command-Esc) and open it again. Version 0.2.128 and older are not guaranteed to upgrade themselves: install this release once by hand — download this DMG and replace the app in Applications, or run the install script again (`curl -fsSL https://droppen.app/install.sh | sh`) — and later releases update themselves as before.

## Droppen App for Linux

The desktop app for Linux, the same app as on the Mac. It needs glibc 2.35 or later (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12 and newer), on x86_64 (`x86_64`, `amd64`) or 64-bit Arm (`aarch64`, `arm64`). The AppImage, `Droppen-0.2.142-x86_64.AppImage` or `Droppen-0.2.142-aarch64.AppImage`, is one file that carries its own libraries and updates itself; the .deb, `droppen_0.2.142_amd64.deb` or `droppen_0.2.142_arm64.deb`, is for Debian and Ubuntu. To install it the first time, keep the AppImage in a folder you can write to, make it executable (`chmod +x Droppen-0.2.142-x86_64.AppImage`) and open it; or install the .deb with `sudo apt install ./droppen_0.2.142_amd64.deb` and open Droppen from your app launcher.

The AppImages of Droppen 0.2.139 to 0.2.141 update themselves to this release: they download this release's AppImage for their architecture in the background (they wait while NetworkManager says the connection is metered), check its signature, and write it over the old file, under the same name, the next time you quit, or at once with *Restart to update*. They do not update where you cannot replace the file (the menu says *Move Droppen to a Folder You Can Write to Update*), nor when they run unpacked (`--appimage-extract-and-run`). The .deb does not update itself: install this release's .deb the same way, `sudo apt install ./droppen_0.2.142_amd64.deb` (`droppen_0.2.142_arm64.deb` on Arm). `Droppen-0.2.142-linux-sources.tar` holds the source of the libraries the AppImages carry and of the AppImage runtime; `LIBRARIES-x86_64.txt` and `LIBRARIES-aarch64.txt` list each library with its Ubuntu package and version.

## What's new in v0.2.142

This release is about slow links (around 1 Mbps, as on a weak mobile connection), from a connection log sent in with v0.2.141. Most of it works best with both ends updated: the page on droppen.app and the Droppen on your device. Against an older device, each side does what it did before.

### On droppen.app

- **Small requests no longer wait behind large transfers.** The page and the device each kept up to 512 KiB in flight on a connection. At 1 Mbps that is more than 4 s of data queued ahead of everything sent after it: a session's next page, the screens and apps the page asks for, and the small probes that check the connection is alive. Now each side keeps only about two round trips' worth in flight, at least 64 KiB, measured from the path it is using, the relay or the direct path. Acknowledgements and probes no longer wait behind data. In tests at 1 Mbps and a 70 ms round trip, requests made during a download came back in 0.5 s instead of 2.1 s (95th percentile), and the download kept 98% of its speed. With large transfers both ways at once, requests took 0.9 s instead of 3.1 s, and each direction kept at least 90% of its speed. On fast links nothing changes.
- **A busy direct path is no longer taken for a dead one.** With a few hundred KB queued on a slow direct path, the page or the device gave the path up after 1.5 s without an acknowledgement, sent what it carried again through the relay, and after a few such drops waited two minutes or more before trying the direct path again. Now how long a side waits depends on how much is queued ahead and how fast the path has carried data lately, up to 8 s. A path that dies while idle is still found within 3.5 s, or 1.5 s when a keystroke is waiting. After a drop under load, the next try comes within 30 s, for up to three drops in a row. With an older device, the device still gives up a busy direct path after 1.5 s.
- **The direct path carrying data keeps the connection open.** Since v0.2.141 the page closes a connection when a relay probe has gone 10 s unanswered and nothing has arrived through the relay for 10 s. It did so even while data was arriving over the direct path, then connected again from the start. Now data arriving over either path keeps the connection.
- **The offline copy gives way to what you are doing.** droppen.app keeps an offline copy of your devices' sessions, downloaded from the Hub in pages, four at a time. On a slow link those downloads took the bandwidth the live connection needed. Now the session you are looking at, and the session list of the device you are looking at, come first. The rest goes one page at a time on a slow link (3G or slower, *Data Saver* on, under 5 Mbps, or a round trip of 250 ms or more). It also pauses while a request to a device is under way, and for 10 s after a connection opens. A page that receives nothing for 30 s is given up and tried again later. Closing a session's copy stops its downloads. On a slow link, the rest of the copy now takes about three times as long to arrive.
- **The connection log says more.** New events for the offline copy's downloads (`stream.checkpoint`, `stream.progress`, `stream.done`) and their pauses (`pages.pause`, `pages.resume`, `pages.limits`), and for a request the device refused (`pipe.refused`). A timeout now logs the limit it used and why (`floorMs`, `loadMs`, `aheadBytes`, `rateBytesPerSec`), and a direct path the device gave up is logged as `path.peerDown`. The once-a-second sample has each path's in-flight limit, rate and minimum round trip. In `hub.req`, `ms` is now `ttfbMs` and `size` is `declaredBytes`, which is what they always measured: the time to the response's headers and the size it declared.

### Sessions

- **An open session no longer resends its state every 2 s.** A session's state includes its recent questions (AskUserQuestion), and an answered question with two or more answers read back in a different order each time. The device took that for a change and sent the whole state again, up to about 40 KB, every 2 s, for as long as a page had the session open. That is about 170 kbit/s, a sixth of a 1 Mbps link, for a session where nothing happened. Now the state is sent only when it changes. This had been so since v0.2.125, in the app and on droppen.app alike.

### For developers

- Nothing changes on the wire. The pacing is the sender's own: `crates/droppen-proto/src/pace.rs` and `web/src/lib/e2e/pace.ts` estimate each path's delivery rate and minimum round trip from ordinary `probe` envelopes sent after data, which every version since v0.2.125 answers at once on the same path. A shared trace in `crates/droppen-proto/vectors/rust.json` pins both implementations to the same decisions. The direct path's deadlines are `directDeadline` in `web/src/lib/e2e/path.ts` and `direct_deadline` in `crates/droppen-proto/src/path.rs`, also pinned by shared vectors. Probes are now limited per path, 16 each, so late probes on one path cannot push out another's. `docs/cross-machine-architecture.md` and `docs/relay-and-plans.md` describe both.
- The server's API: a session question's `answers` is now serialised with its keys in sorted order. Its JSON shape is unchanged.

## v0.2.142 更新内容

这个版本针对慢速链路（大约 1 Mbps，比如信号差的手机网络），起因是一份用 v0.2.141 发来的连接日志。大部分改进要两端都更新才最好：droppen.app 上的页面，以及你设备上的 Droppen。设备还是旧版本时，两边各自照旧。

### droppen.app 上

- **小请求不再排在大传输后面。** 页面和设备以前在一个连接上最多各有 512 KiB 在途。在 1 Mbps 下，这是 4 秒多的数据排在之后发出的一切前面：会话的下一页、页面要的屏幕和应用列表，还有检查连接是否还活着的小探测。现在每一方只保留大约两个往返的在途量，至少 64 KiB，按它正在用的路径（中继或直连）测量。确认和探测不再排在数据后面。在 1 Mbps、70 ms 往返的测试里，下载期间发出的请求 0.5 秒就回来，而不是 2.1 秒（第 95 百分位），下载仍保持 98% 的速度。两个方向同时有大传输时，请求用 0.9 秒而不是 3.1 秒，每个方向都保持至少 90% 的速度。快速链路上没有变化。
- **忙碌的直连不再被当成断开。** 慢速直连上排着几百 KB 时，页面或设备以前在 1.5 秒收不到确认后就放弃这条路径，把它在传的数据经中继重发一遍；这样断几次之后，要等两分钟甚至更久才再试直连。现在一方等多久，取决于前面排了多少数据、这条路径最近传得多快，最多 8 秒。空闲时断掉的路径仍在 3.5 秒内发现，有按键在等时是 1.5 秒。负载下断开后，下一次尝试在 30 秒内，最多连续三次。设备还是旧版本时，设备那边仍会在 1.5 秒后放弃忙碌的直连。
- **直连在传数据时，连接不再被关掉。** 从 v0.2.141 起，一个中继探测 10 秒没有回答、并且 10 秒里中继上什么都没收到时，页面就关闭连接。即使数据正经直连源源不断地到达，它也会这样做，然后从头重新连接。现在经任何一条路径到达的数据都能让连接保持。
- **离线副本给你正在做的事让路。** droppen.app 会为你设备上的会话保留一份离线副本，从 Hub 分页下载，每次 4 页。在慢速链路上，这些下载会占掉实时连接需要的带宽。现在你正在看的会话，以及你正在看的设备的会话列表优先下载。其余的在慢速链路上（3G 或更慢、开了「省流量」、低于 5 Mbps，或往返 250 ms 以上）一次只下载一页，并且在有请求正发往设备时、以及连接打开后的 10 秒内暂停。一页 30 秒什么都没收到就放弃，稍后再试。关闭一个会话的副本会停止它的下载。在慢速链路上，副本其余部分到齐所需的时间大约变成原来的三倍。
- **连接日志记得更多。** 新增离线副本下载的事件（`stream.checkpoint`、`stream.progress`、`stream.done`）和它们的暂停（`pages.pause`、`pages.resume`、`pages.limits`），以及设备拒绝的请求（`pipe.refused`）。超时现在会记下它用的时限和原因（`floorMs`、`loadMs`、`aheadBytes`、`rateBytesPerSec`），设备放弃直连记为 `path.peerDown`。每秒一次的采样里有每条路径的在途上限、速率和最小往返。`hub.req` 里的 `ms` 改名为 `ttfbMs`，`size` 改名为 `declaredBytes`，这才是它们一直测量的东西：到响应头的时间，以及响应声明的大小。

### 会话

- **打开的会话不再每 2 秒重发一次状态。** 会话的状态里包含它最近的提问（AskUserQuestion），而回答了两项及以上的提问，每次读出来答案的顺序都不一样。设备把这当成状态变了，于是只要有页面开着这个会话，就每 2 秒把整份状态重发一次，最多约 40 KB。这大约是 170 kbit/s，占掉 1 Mbps 链路的六分之一，而这个会话什么都没发生。现在状态只在真的变化时才发送。这个问题从 v0.2.125 起就存在，App 和 droppen.app 上都有。

### 面向开发者

- 线上格式没有任何变化。限速完全由发送方自己做：`crates/droppen-proto/src/pace.rs` 和 `web/src/lib/e2e/pace.ts` 根据数据后面发出的普通 `probe` 信封，估算每条路径的送达速率和最小往返；v0.2.125 起的每个版本都会在同一路径上立即回答这些信封。`crates/droppen-proto/vectors/rust.json` 里的共享轨迹保证两边实现做出相同的决定。直连的时限是 `web/src/lib/e2e/path.ts` 的 `directDeadline` 和 `crates/droppen-proto/src/path.rs` 的 `direct_deadline`，同样由共享向量固定。探测现在按路径各自限额，每条路径 16 个，一条路径上迟到的探测不会挤掉另一条路径的。`docs/cross-machine-architecture.md` 和 `docs/relay-and-plans.md` 介绍了这两部分。
- 服务端 API：会话提问的 `answers` 现在按键排序后序列化，JSON 结构不变。
